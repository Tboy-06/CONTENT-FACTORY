## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. In August 1947, the end of two centuries of British rule triggered the largest and bloodiest forced migration in human history.
2. As the sun set on the British Raj, an estimated one million people lost their lives in a sudden, violent upheaval.
3. For centuries prior, the Indian subcontinent thrived as a complex mosaic of regional kingdoms and princely states.
4. This diverse land was home to a tapestry of faiths, including Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists, Christians, Parsis, and Jews.
5. Every region maintained its own unique traditions, caste structures, and local leadership.
6. But beneath this cultural harmony, the pressures of colonial policy and shifting political identities began to fracture the foundation of the subcontinent.
7. The partition was not merely a border adjustment, but a traumatic severance that redefined millions of lives overnight.
8. Ultimately, the split of India stands as a grim reminder of how colonial legacies can permanently alter the course of human history.
