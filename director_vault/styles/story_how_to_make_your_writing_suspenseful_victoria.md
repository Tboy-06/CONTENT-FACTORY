## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. True terror is not found in the monsters we see, but in the ones we imagine.
2. Many writers mistakenly believe that graphic violence and jump scares are the foundation of a great horror story.
3. However, H.P. Lovecraft famously noted that the oldest and strongest kind of fear is the fear of the unknown.
4. To master suspense, you must resist the urge to reveal your horrors too quickly.
5. True tension is built by keeping your audience trapped in a state of constant, agonizing anticipation.
6. By withholding information, you force the reader to fill the silence with their own worst nightmares.
7. Suspense is the psychological gap between what a character knows and what the audience fears is coming next.
8. Great writing keeps the reader on edge by focusing on the threat of the unknown rather than the reveal of the monster.
