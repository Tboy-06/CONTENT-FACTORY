## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Inciting Anomaly
1. Do you ever feel like you're just floating through
2. life...but not actually getting closer 
to the person that you want to be.
3. It usually happens around New years, you imagine 
all the bad habits your going to break free from,
4. and all the good habits you will begin.
5. “This time it will be different” 
you say to yourself. This time I
6. AM going to do the things that I say I will.

### Act 2: Hidden Mechanism
1. Over time, rewards become associated with cues.
2. So, in this example, checking social media 
becomes tied to your mind going blank at work.
3. And then checking Facebook may be the cue to check 
Instagram, which becomes the cue to check YouTube.
4. And before you know it, your mind going blank 
cue has led to 20 minutes of wasted time.
5. And you more you repeat these habit loops, 
the stronger and more automatic they become.
6. Cues can really be anything. A smell, a 
sound a sight, a person, a location etc.

### Act 3: Crisis/Climax
1. People who have the underlying motive of 
connecting with others may jump onto Facebook,
2. others seeking the underlying motive of finding 
love and reproducing may sign up for Tinder.
3. Reducing uncertainty, there’s Google for that. 
Seeking social acceptance, there is Instagram.
4. Reprogramming your brain to enjoy hard habits
5. “You can make hard habits more attractive if you
6. can learn to associate them 
with a positive experience.”

### Act 4: Aftermath
1. Ultimately, when you find the habits you 
want to work on. you want to be pushing
2. Desired good habits towards this side of the 
spectrum, and bad habits towards this side.
3. For the working out habit.
4. The first step was to make the cues more obvious, 
and I had a few tools I could use from the book.
5. In this case I used what James Clear 
calls the implementation strategy.
6. I will workout at 6am in the living room.
