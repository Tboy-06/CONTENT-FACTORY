## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. True leadership is defined not by the power you hold, but by the level of safety you provide for those under your command.
2. On September 8, 2009, Captain William Swenson found himself in the middle of a brutal, three-sided ambush in Afghanistan.
3. While leading a column of American and Afghan troops to protect local government officials, his unit suddenly faced overwhelming enemy fire.
4. In the absolute chaos of the battlefield, the difference between life and death often rests on the actions of a single, courageous leader.
5. Swenson’s instinctive response to the crisis ultimately earned him the Congressional Medal of Honor for his heroic bravery.
6. His story highlights a fundamental truth that Simon Sinek champions: the best leaders prioritize the security of their people above their own survival.
7. When a leader creates an environment of safety, they empower their team to achieve the impossible under the most intense pressure.
8. Ultimately, the greatest leaders are those who are willing to sacrifice their own interests to ensure the people they lead return home safely.
