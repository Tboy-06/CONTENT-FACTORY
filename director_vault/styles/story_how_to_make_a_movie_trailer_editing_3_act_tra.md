## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. The secret to a blockbuster trailer isn't just flashy cuts; it's a structural masterpiece that demands the audience's attention in under three minutes.
2. Professional editors like Derek Lieu know that trailers function as miniature films, mirroring the classic three-act narrative structure used in Hollywood features.
3. Just as a movie must establish its world, a trailer needs to quickly orient the viewer, whether it’s a gritty drama or a multiverse-spanning animation.
4. By using relatable analogies, like Peter B. Parker explaining his reality through a simple fry, trailers transform complex high-concept plots into digestible hooks.
5. Every trailer follows a deliberate line of rising action, carefully calibrating the dramatic tension to keep the viewer locked in from the opening frame.
6. While the pacing is significantly faster than a feature film, the goal remains the same: to create a psychological bridge between the viewer and the story.
7. Mastering this pacing requires an editor to identify the most compelling moments that define the stakes of the universe within a condensed timeline.
8. Ultimately, the architecture of a great trailer relies on a calculated escalation that forces the audience to crave the full story by the time the screen goes black.
