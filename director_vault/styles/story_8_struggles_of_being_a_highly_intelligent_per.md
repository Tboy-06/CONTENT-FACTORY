## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. Intelligence is far more than a static IQ score, yet for those who possess it, this cognitive gift often arrives with a heavy, hidden burden.
2. Even legendary minds like Stephen Hawking rejected simplistic labeling, proving that true intellect is a complex, multi-dimensional phenomenon that defies easy categorization.
3. Highly intelligent people frequently struggle with chronic overthinking, as their brains are wired to analyze every possible variable of a situation.
4. This constant mental processing often leads to social isolation, as finding peers who match their depth of thought can feel like an impossible search.
5. They are prone to imposter syndrome, constantly doubting their own competence despite clear evidence of their remarkable mental agility.
6. The tendency to perfectionism serves as a double-edged sword, driving excellence while simultaneously inviting paralyzing anxiety over the smallest perceived errors.
7. Because they anticipate outcomes so vividly, they often experience deep existential dread that others might simply overlook.
8. Ultimately, the very brilliance that separates them from the crowd is the same force that makes navigating a standardized world feel like a constant, uphill battle.
