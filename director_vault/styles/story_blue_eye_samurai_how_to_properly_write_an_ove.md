## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. If you want to master the art of writing an overpowered protagonist, you need to stop looking at modern blockbusters and start watching Blue Eye Samurai.
2. On the surface, Mizu appears to be a walking cliché—an absurdly skilled warrior who repeatedly triumphs against insurmountable odds.
3. In lesser hands, this level of dominance would render a story stakes-free and ultimately boring.
4. However, the show avoids these common pitfalls by framing Mizu’s lethality as a burden rather than a superpower.
5. Every victory comes at a grueling physical cost, ensuring she is constantly pushed to the literal brink of death.
6. Her internal struggle and deep-seated trauma serve as a counterweight to her unmatched blade work, grounding her in painful humanity.
7. By stripping away the typical 'chosen one' armor, the narrative forces us to root for her survival instead of just her inevitable victory.
8. Ultimately, an overpowered character only works when their greatest challenge is not the enemy they face, but the broken self they cannot escape.
