## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. Hollywood has conditioned us to crave the predictable comfort of a perfect happy ending, but Studio Ghibli dares to offer something far more profound.
2. While Western blockbusters rely on rigid narrative arcs where every problem is neatly solved, Hayao Miyazaki’s masterpieces operate under an entirely different set of rules.
3. Critics often label these films as weird, unsettling, or even aimless, missing the beauty in their intentional ambiguity.
4. Instead of rushing toward a definitive climax, Ghibli films invite us to linger in the quiet, atmospheric pockets of existence.
5. This departure from standard convention isn’t a lack of direction; it is a deliberate choice to mirror the messy, unresolved nature of real life.
6. When a story refuses to provide a tidy resolution, it forces the audience to engage with the themes rather than just the plot points.
7. There is a unique, refreshing power in narratives that prioritize internal growth over external victory.
8. By rejecting the constraints of the traditional happy ending, Ghibli teaches us that the journey itself is the only destination that truly matters.
