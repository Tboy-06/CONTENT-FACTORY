# Story Structure: 4 Rules for Writing NARRATION

## Narrative Overview

- Total Duration: 765.0s
- Hook: "Screenwriting professors universally despise narration in student scripts, but the problem isn't the technique itself—it is the execution."
- Payoff: "Effective narration should never compete with your visuals; it should exist to provide the secret, silent rhythm of your character's soul."

## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. Screenwriting professors universally despise narration in student scripts, but the problem isn't the technique itself—it is the execution.
2. Most amateur writers fall into the trap of using voiceover to state the obvious, explaining exactly what the audience is already watching on screen.
3. Consider a scene where a character says, 'I made some eggs, I was starving, it’s almost noon,' while the audience clearly sees a man cooking breakfast.
4. This redundant narration adds nothing to the story, serving only to bore the viewer and insult their intelligence.
5. Great narration, by contrast, creates a cognitive gap between what we hear and what we see, providing subtext rather than a play-by-play.
6. To master this, you must learn to use the voiceover to reveal the character's internal desires or contradictions that remain hidden from their physical actions.
7. By shifting the focus from descriptive observation to emotional depth, you transform a tired script element into a powerful storytelling tool.
8. Effective narration should never compete with your visuals; it should exist to provide the secret, silent rhythm of your character's soul.
