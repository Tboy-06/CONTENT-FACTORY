## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. In the world of television, even a one‑minute misstep in a pilot can mean the difference between a hit and a humming void of indifference.
2. Audiences often skim an episode, and if a show can't hook them in the first few minutes, they’ll say, "I heard you did a pilot. That was my fifteen minutes."
3. That brutal feedback underscores why the most successful pilots start with a compelling hook that demands attention immediately.
4. Behind that bravery lies a simple causality rule that has guided creators like Trey Parker and Matt Stone for decades.
5. Instead of a list of events, the rule demands a chain of causes and consequences where every action propels the plot forward.
6. A pilot should feel like a well‑tuned domino set: one decision throws the next into motion, revealing the world’s logic instantly.
7. Testing this continuity in rehearsal can turn a promising idea into a story that feels inevitable, decided by the characters before the audience even asks why.
8. By committing to cause‑effect from the very first beat, writers can craft pilots that sell, not just sellable, but irresistible.
