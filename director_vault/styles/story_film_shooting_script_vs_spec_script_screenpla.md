## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. If you are an aspiring screenwriter hoping to sell your work, you must first master the crucial distinction between a spec script and a shooting script.
2. A spec script—short for speculation—is a screenplay written entirely on your own initiative without a prior commission or payment.
3. Most industry screenplays you find online are actually production drafts, which are vastly different from the spec format you need to master.
4. These shooting scripts contain technical production details meant for the crew that will only clutter and ruin your professional spec submission.
5. When you write on speculation, your primary goal is to tell a compelling story that stands on its own merits without needing extra guidance.
6. Novice writers often sabotage their chances by confusing these two formats and including camera angles or editing cues that producers simply do not want to see.
7. Learning the nuances of professional formatting ensures that your voice remains the priority while you capture the attention of busy industry executives.
8. Understanding that a spec script is a blueprint for a story rather than a technical manual is the first step toward getting your screenplay produced.
