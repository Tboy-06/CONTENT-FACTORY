## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. Fear is a universal human experience, making it one of the most powerful tools a writer can use to define a character.
2. Across all forms of media, cowardice frequently drives the emotional core of iconic stories and unforgettable character arcs.
3. However, crafting a character who is constantly paralyzed by terror is an exceptionally delicate tightrope for creators to walk.
4. When written with nuance, a coward's vulnerability creates immediate empathy and sets the stage for meaningful personal growth.
5. Yet when handled poorly, that exact same fear degrades from understandable self-preservation into whiny, repetitive frustration for the audience.
6. The true distinction between a sympathetic coward and an insufferable nuisance lies in whether their fear generates passive resistance or active choices.
7. By examining how stories explore the archetype of the coward, we uncover the exact mechanics of compelling character design.
8. Ultimately, masterful storytelling proves that an afraid character invites deep empathy, while a coward written without purpose only breeds annoyance.
