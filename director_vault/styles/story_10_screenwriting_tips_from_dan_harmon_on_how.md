## Acts & Narrative Breakdown
### Act 1: Full Narrative Arc
1. Dan Harmon’s genius doesn't lie in brilliant concepts, but in his relentless obsession with the emotional architecture behind them.
2. When a writer pitched the absurd idea of Rick turning himself into a pickle, Harmon didn't just chase the shock value of the gag.
3. He immediately pivoted to the character’s internal motivation, insisting that the most ridiculous scenarios require the most complex emotional justification.
4. Harmon explicitly rejects 'idea worship,' arguing that chasing a 'good idea' is a common trap that distracts from authentic storytelling.
5. He views television not as a vehicle for clever concepts, but as a comfort tool designed to sustain long-term investment.
6. By breaking down characters like Jeff Winger into their base, relatable human parts, he constructs narratives that feel deeply grounded despite their chaotic settings.
7. The secret to his success is realizing that the audience stays for the character’s journey, not the premise of the episode.
8. True mastery in screenwriting comes from ensuring that even your most outlandish moments are fueled by honest, human struggle.
