> "Another important thing to remember is that you cannot edit AI. Say you just wanted that overhead scene of the cop and raccoon. Maybe you gave it a clean storyboard without the blue line and prompted them to chase each other in an overhead shot. It might get that right, but then it will also change several other things--the color of the floor, or the sound effects, or the cop's uniform is different, etc. Now you're two prompts in, and your tokens/credits are dwindling. You can try to prompt again, but is it worth rolling the dice? 

Same goes for the transformers thing. Maybe you prompt it again to make sure all the cars are going the correct way, and it does that, but decides to make all the cars the exact same make and model, or it makes the cars in the opposite lane also go in the same direction. And once again, you're losing credits (aka actual money) every time you try again. You can't "lock in" things that you really like, because the AI will change things even if you tell it not to. 

With traditional processes and real humans, you can get exactly what you want, and with CGI and animation, you can go back and tweak very specific things without messing anything else up. I had to make a lot of AI videos at my last job and I hated it, because the best you could ever get was mediocre. And that's yet another problem with AI video: it conditions us to accept mediocrity." — @commenterica
