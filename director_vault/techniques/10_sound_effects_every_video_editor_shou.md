> "1. Whoosh (0:50): Perfect for fast transitions, quick object movements across the screen, or dynamic title reveals.
2. Swoosh (1:23): A softer, smoother version of the whoosh, ideal for UI animations or subtle motion graphics.
3. Riser (2:02): Essential for building tension or anticipation before a jump scare, a big reveal, or a music drop.
4. Ambient Sounds (2:16): Adds realism to a scene (e.g., traffic or birds). Use these to create a believable atmosphere so a video doesn't feel unnaturally silent.
5. Motion Sound Effects (3:06): These provide feedback for small, real-life movements like walking, dragging items, or flipping pages, adding a layer of immersion.
6. Cartoon Sound Effects (5:00): Used for lighthearted or funny content (e.g., pops, slides, or boings) to emphasize humor.
7. Emotion-Elevating Sounds (5:33): Includes sounds like heartbeats, heavy breathing, or clock ticking. Use these to heighten anxiety, tension, or intimacy.
8. Hit/Impact (6:09): Best for dramatic emphasis on a scene, often used in trailers or high-impact moments to add power.
9. Whip (6:38): Useful for fast cuts, reaction shots, or comedic timing to provide a sharp, snappy transition.
10.Foley (7:05): Sounds recorded in a studio to mimic real life (e.g., footsteps or fabric rustling). Use these to create realism when natural sound is missing." (at 0:50) — @Adityatiwari-h5i
