> "When people use the term "cinematic", which cinema or which movies are they referring to?  
In reality, there are many factors that go into "the look" of a movie:  Control over lighting, location, wardrobe, makeup, video sensor or film emulsion that's chosen, filtration, color grading, etc.   And those choices cover a very wide range and will likely differ substantially from one production to the next, and with very different results! What differs between a movie that's been crafted by seasoned filmmakers as opposed to lesser experienced filmmakers is this:  Intentionality. The thing that stands out about movies, especially high end productions, is that they tend to be highly stylized, with the end result being the outcome of a series of very deliberate and subjective choices. So when you hear someone say "cinematic", don't imagine that it's a specific series of steps, LUTs, node structures, color or contrast choices, or that achieving a better result is even restricted to post production; instead, understand that a "filmic" or "cinematic" look is when you make very deliberate, subjective choices about what goes into the look of a project, from beginning to end. And if an audience ends up liking the choices that were made... in actuality, that's when a movie or a show becomes "cinematic"!" — @Orvulum
